Safety

Last updated: 17 October 2026

Flashing light (photosensitive seizures)

Sky Waker shows flashing lights, bright bursts and fast patterns timed to music. In a small number of people, these can cause seizures, even in people who have never had one or been told they have epilepsy.

If you have photosensitive epilepsy, or flashing images have made you unwell before, turn on Reduce flashing before you start. Ask a doctor first if you are unsure.

Stop at once, take the headset off and see a doctor if you or someone watching notices any of these:

  • dizziness, light-headedness or feeling sick;
  • twitching of the eyes or muscles, or movements you can't control;
  • blurred or changed vision;
  • confusion, or losing track of where you are.

Reduce flashing

Turn it on in the lobby under System, in the VJ's timeline bar, or on the notice shown before your first Enter. It keeps bright flashes to three a second or fewer and softens them. It lowers the risk; it does not remove every risk. Turned on in the VJ, it applies to every screen and headset playing that show. Turned on in the lobby or before Enter, it applies to that browser or headset.

Using a headset

  • Clear the space around you: furniture, stairs, pets and other people. Keep passthrough on so you can see the room.
  • Take a break every 30 minutes, and stop if you feel sick, dizzy or your eyes are tired.
  • Follow your headset maker's safety and age guidance.
  • Don't use a headset while driving or moving, or anywhere you need to pay attention to your surroundings.

Shows with an audience

If you play Sky Waker for other people, tell them before the show that it contains flashing light, and keep Reduce flashing on unless you know your audience.
